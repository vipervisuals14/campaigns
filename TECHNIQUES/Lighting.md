# TECHNIQUE — Lighting

**Purpose:** Match natural lighting so the subject and environment read as one photographic moment.
**Last updated:** 2026-11-18

---

## 1. Core concepts

- **Direction** — where the dominant light comes from (front, side, back, top).
- **Intensity** — exposure relationship between subject and environment.
- **Quality** — hard (direct sun) vs. soft (overcast/diffused) and the resulting shadow edge.
- **Colour temperature** — warm (tungsten/sunrise) vs. cool (shade/overcast), measured in Kelvin.
- **Ambient / fill** — light bounced from the environment onto the subject.
- **Reflected light** — colour and light bounced from nearby surfaces.

## 2. Analysis workflow

1. Determine the dominant light direction from highlights and shadows in the original.
2. Estimate colour temperature from neutral surfaces.
3. Note shadow softness and length.
4. Identify secondary/ambient sources (sky, bounce, reflectors).
5. Record the environment's light so the reconstruction is consistent.

## 3. Matching rules

- Match the environment's light direction to the original photo before integrating the subject.
- Match colour temperature across subject and environment; adjust in a colour-managed workflow.
- Preserve authentic lighting transitions — no artificial-looking gradients.
- Do not introduce dramatic/cinematic lighting that the original did not contain.

## 4. Common mistakes

| Symptom | Cause |
|---|---|
| Subject looks pasted | Colour temperature or exposure mismatch |
| Flat, lifeless subject | Missing ambient fill / bounce |
| Overcooked highlights | Excessive contrast or HDR |
| Wrong shadow direction | Light direction not matched to environment |

## 5. Verification

- Sample neutral grey/white in subject and environment and compare Kelvin.
- Check highlight rolloff and shadow density side by side.
- Confirm the subject's lit side agrees with the environment's lit side.
